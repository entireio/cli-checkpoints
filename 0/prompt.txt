## CONFIRMED DEFECTS

### 1. The attribution rule is never stated, and the plan's showcase numbers come from a rule that contradicts the code it harvests. *(blocking)*

**Claim:** Task 2 Step 3 — "Implement the aggregator. Group by label." Step 4 — conservation: "the sum of every row's tokens plus the not-recorded count equals the report's own total."

**Found:** `CallUsage.Consumed` is `[]ToolResultRef` — a call can consume several *different* tools. Nothing in the plan says how one call's usage divides among them, or where cache reads go. Those are the whole design, and the two available answers give completely different reports on the very checkpoint the plan showcases:

- The **spike** that produced the measurements (`/tmp/pr3-probe/cmd/entire/cli/zz_spike_attribution.go`) assigns `input+cacheCreation+cacheRead+output` of the whole call to the **first** consumed ref and `break`s.
- The **harvestable** engine (`origin/tokens-report:cmd/entire/cli/tokenreport/attribution.go`) does something else entirely: output/thinking → the refs the call **Emitted** (equal split, largest remainder); input/cache-writes → the refs it **Consumed** (proportional to `Bytes`); and **`CacheReadTokens` of every call → a single `LabelContextReplay` row.**

On `01M158H55KBAESQZCWX9VQ5D2P`, cache read is 87.6% of the 111M. Under the harvested rule the table is one ~97M "context replay" row and tool rows of ≤3%. The plan's table shows `(no tool) 25%`, `Bash head 15%`, `Bash python3 14%`, `Bash tail 9%` — only reachable by pushing cache reads out onto tool rows. So the advertised output is an artifact of the spike's first-ref rule, and an implementer following Task 2 as written is as likely to produce the replay-dominated table.

**Correction:** state the rule explicitly in a Decision — where cache reads go, how a multi-tool call splits, and whether output attributes to Emitted or Consumed — and re-derive the showcase numbers under it. Note also that Task 2 Step 1's fixture ("two calls *consuming* a Bash ref … merged into one row with **summed usage**") only makes sense if output attributes to Consumed, which the harvested engine denies.
